Fired Without Cause? Here Is What the Employment Act Says You Are Owed

7 min read March 24, 2026 Victor Muatine

Every week, Kenyans are dismissed from employment without being told why, without receiving their dues, and without understanding their rights. If this has happened to you, here is what the law says you are owed — and what you should do about it.

Unfair Termination vs Summary Dismissal: What Is the Difference?

Unfair termination (governed by Section 45 of the Employment Act, 2007) occurs when an employer ends your contract without a valid reason or without following a fair procedure. Summary dismissal (Section 44) is an immediate, no-notice termination that is only lawful in specific circumstances — gross misconduct, criminal acts, wilful disobedience of lawful orders, or conduct fundamentally incompatible with continued employment.

Employers frequently use "summary dismissal" as a cover for what is actually an unfair termination. If your employer used the words "summary dismissal" but the reason does not meet the Section 44 threshold, your dismissal is likely unlawful regardless of what they called it.

The Governing Law

The Employment Act, 2007 (Cap 226) is Kenya's primary employment statute. Claims for wrongful dismissal are heard by the Employment and Labour Relations Court (ELRC), established by the Employment and Labour Relations Court Act, 2011. You must file your claim within three years of the cause of action.

What the Law Requires Before You Are Dismissed

Section 45 of the Employment Act requires that before terminating your contract, your employer must:

  • Have a valid reason for the termination relating to your conduct, capacity, or the employer's operational requirements
  • Give you a fair hearing — notify you of the reason, give you an opportunity to respond, and genuinely consider your response before deciding
  • Give you proper notice or pay in lieu of notice (unless it is a lawful summary dismissal)

Many employers skip the fair hearing entirely. Even if there is a valid reason for dismissal, failure to follow a fair procedure makes the termination unfair and entitles you to compensation.

Your Entitlements on Termination

Whether your dismissal is fair or not, you are entitled to certain terminal dues. Do not leave without these:

  • Outstanding salary — all wages earned up to the date of termination, including any overtime
  • Accrued but untaken leave — Section 28 entitles you to 21 working days of annual leave per year. Any unused leave days must be paid out
  • Notice pay — if you were not given notice. Section 35 specifies: 28 days for monthly-paid employees, or the period agreed in your contract if longer
  • Service pay — if you are not a member of a pension/provident fund scheme, Section 35(6) entitles you to a gratuity of 15 days' pay for each completed year of service

What You Can Claim for Unfair Dismissal

If your termination was unfair, you can go to the ELRC and claim:

  • Reinstatement — you get your job back, with back pay for the period you were out
  • Compensation — the court may award up to 12 months' gross salary under Section 49(1)(c) of the Employment Act, in addition to your terminal dues
  • The court will consider factors like your length of service, the circumstances of dismissal, and any contribution you made to the termination

"An employer cannot dismiss you at will in Kenya. The Employment Act creates a floor of rights that every worker is entitled to, regardless of what the employment contract says. Any contract clause that purports to remove those rights is void."

The Special Case of Redundancy

Redundancy — where your role is eliminated for economic or structural reasons — is a lawful ground for termination, but it comes with strict procedural requirements under Section 40 of the Employment Act. Your employer must give you one month's written notice, notify the Cabinet Secretary for Labour in writing, and pay you one month's salary or 15 days for each year of service, whichever is greater. Failure to follow this procedure converts a lawful redundancy into an unlawful dismissal.

Practical Steps If You Have Been Dismissed

  1. 1
    Collect and preserve evidence

    Secure copies of your employment contract, payslips, the dismissal letter, and any correspondence about the dismissal. Screenshots of emails or WhatsApp messages are admissible. Do this before your employer can deny you access.

  2. 2
    Demand your terminal dues in writing

    Write to your employer (or HR) immediately. A formal written demand creates a paper trail and starts the clock on their obligation to respond.

  3. 3
    Consult an advocate

    Employment and Labour Relations Court proceedings have their own rules and timelines. An advocate can assess whether you have a strong case, negotiate a settlement, or file a claim on your behalf. Many matters settle before full hearing.

  4. 4
    File at the ELRC within 3 years

    Do not delay. The three-year limitation period under Section 90 of the Employment Act is strict. Courts are reluctant to extend it.
