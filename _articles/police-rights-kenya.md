## Article 49 — Your Constitutional Shield

The moment you are **arrested or detained**, the Constitution of Kenya 2010 kicks in immediately. Under **Article 49**, you have the right to:

- Be **informed promptly** of the reason for your arrest — in a language you understand
- **Remain silent** and be warned that anything you say may be used against you
- **Communicate** with an advocate and any person you choose
- **Not be compelled** to make a confession or admission
- Be **brought before a court within 24 hours** of arrest (or 48 hours if a court is not reasonably accessible)

## What to Do in the First 5 Minutes

1. **Stay calm** — do not resist physically, even if the arrest is unlawful
2. **Ask clearly**: "Am I under arrest or am I free to go?"
3. **State your right to silence**: "I am exercising my right to remain silent until I speak with a lawyer"
4. **Do not sign anything** without legal advice
5. **Memorise or discreetly note** the officer's badge number and the time of arrest

## If Your Rights Are Violated

A violation of constitutional rights during arrest does not automatically get you released, but it has serious consequences for the prosecution's case. Evidence obtained through torture or coercion is inadmissible under **Article 50(4)**.

You can also file a **petition in the High Court** for violation of fundamental rights under Article 22, and seek compensation under Article 23.

Know your rights. Exercise them calmly. Then call a lawyer.
