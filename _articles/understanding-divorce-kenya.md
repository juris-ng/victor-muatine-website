## The Legal Grounds for Divorce in Kenya

Under the **Matrimonial Causes Act (Cap 152)**, a marriage may be dissolved on the ground that it has **irretrievably broken down**. Kenyan courts look at specific facts to establish this — including adultery, cruelty, desertion for at least three years, and separation.

## The Divorce Process

1. **Filing a Petition** — Either spouse files a divorce petition at the High Court or Magistrate's Court depending on the nature of the marriage
2. **Service of Petition** — The other spouse is formally served with court papers
3. **Response** — The respondent has 21 days to file an answer
4. **Hearing** — The court hears evidence from both sides
5. **Decree Nisi** — A conditional divorce order is granted
6. **Decree Absolute** — After three months, the divorce becomes final

## Children and Custody

The court's primary consideration is always the **best interests of the child**. Neither parent has an automatic right to custody. Courts consider the child's age, health, emotional attachment, and the ability of each parent to provide stable care.

## Division of Matrimonial Property

The **Matrimonial Property Act 2013** governs how property is divided. Both spouses have a right to share in matrimonial property proportionate to their contribution — financial or otherwise, including domestic work and child-rearing.
