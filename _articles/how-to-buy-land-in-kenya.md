Land fraud remains one of the most prevalent legal disputes in Kenya. Every year, thousands of Kenyans lose their hard-earned money to fraudulent land transactions. Knowing what to do before you sign anything is not optional — it is essential.

## 1. Conduct a Search at the Lands Registry

The first step in any land purchase is conducting an official search at the **National Lands Commission** or the relevant county lands office. This search will reveal:

- The **registered owner** of the parcel
- Whether the land has any **cautions, caveats, or charges** registered against it
- The **nature of the title** (freehold, leasehold, or sectional)

> A land search costs approximately KES 500 and takes 1–3 working days. Never skip this step, no matter how trustworthy the seller appears.

## 2. Verify the Title Deed

Once you have the search results, verify the title deed by:

- Checking that the **LR number** on the deed matches the search results
- Confirming the **names and ID numbers** match those on the seller's identification documents
- Ensuring the title is **not subject to any caution or injunction**

## 3. Conduct a Physical Survey

Before committing any funds, hire a **registered surveyor** to physically beacon the land and confirm:

- The parcel **boundaries** match what is shown on the title
- There are **no encroachments** from neighbouring parcels
- The land is **not on a road reserve, riparian land, or government land**

## 4. Engage an Advocate

Under the **Law Society of Kenya Scale of Fees**, conveyancing transactions require the involvement of an advocate. Your advocate will:

- Draft or review the **Sale Agreement**
- Conduct all due diligence on your behalf
- Prepare the **Transfer Instrument**
- Ensure stamp duty is paid and the title is registered in your name

## 5. Stamp Duty and Registration

Once the sale agreement is executed and full payment made:

- Pay **stamp duty** (4% of land value for urban land, 2% for rural land)
- Have the **Transfer Instrument** executed and witnessed
- Register the transfer at the **Lands Registry**

Only when the title is registered in your name is the transaction legally complete.
