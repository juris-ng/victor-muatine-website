The Story

Victor Muatine is an Advocate of the High Court of Kenya whose name has become synonymous with bold, fearless advocacy. From early in his career, Victor distinguished himself not just as a competent lawyer, but as a passionate voice for clients who needed someone to fight unapologetically in their corner.

Armed with a Bachelor of Laws (LLB) from the University of Nairobi — one of Kenya's most competitive law programmes — and a Postgraduate Diploma from the Kenya School of Law, Victor was called to the bar with a clear purpose: to use the law as a tool for justice, not just procedure.

His public profile grew significantly when a video of him passionately arguing a paternity case at Kisumu Children's Court went viral — capturing the attention of Kenyans who recognised in him an advocate who genuinely cares about outcomes. That authenticity is not performance. It is who Victor Muatine is, in every court, in every matter, for every client.

Philosophy

Victor founded Victor Muatine & Co. Advocates on three non-negotiable pillars: Integrity in every engagement, Strategy in every argument, and Results for every client. The firm does not measure success by the number of cases filed — it measures it by outcomes delivered.

He believes deeply that access to quality legal representation should not be a privilege of the wealthy. Every person — regardless of background — deserves an advocate who will stand and fight. This conviction shapes how the firm engages with every prospective client from the very first consultation.

Beyond the Courtroom

Victor runs a YouTube channel (@publaw) and Instagram (@publ_aw) dedicated to legal education — breaking down complex Kenyan law into accessible content for ordinary citizens. He is a firm believer that an informed public makes for a more just society, and that lawyers have a duty to share knowledge beyond the courtroom walls.

Known online simply as "Professor Muatine," his content reaches thousands of Kenyans who would otherwise have no practical access to legal guidance. This commitment to public legal education is as central to his identity as his work in court.
